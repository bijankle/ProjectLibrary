# KCGM Flashcards

Full screen, offline capable swipe flashcard deck on the KCGM Fimiston processing plant (Growth Project, Stage 1 and Stage 2).
Built for learning the plant language: area codes, tag numbering, circuit logic, acronyms and shift handover talk.

Open it at https://bijankle.github.io/KCGMhomework/ and use Chrome's "Add to Home screen" so it launches full screen like an app.

To add cards, edit `cards.js` (each card has a question `q`, a short answer `a` and the detail `e`),
then bump `VERSION` in `sw.js` so phones pick up the change.

## Follow up questions (AI)

On the answer side of each card there is a "Dig deeper" section with three suggested follow up questions and a box for your own.
Answers come from Google Gemini using your own free key from aistudio.google.com (Settings on the home screen). The key is stored only on your phone.
Every question is sent with `facts.js` (a condensed plant reference from the design criteria and control philosophies), the glossary and the cards in the same topic,
and each answer is tagged as coming from the plant docs, docs plus general knowledge, general knowledge only, or not covered.

## Interactive PFD (desktop)

On a computer the site opens `pfd.html`, a one page interactive PFD of the plant at major equipment level (use `index.html?cards` for the flashcards; phones get the flashcards with a View PFD button).
Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, click equipment or streams for design data, control notes and connections. Flow buttons animate and step through the main line,
grinding loops, flotation, concentrate and gold path, carbon loop, water and reagents. Toggles show control loop badges, service lines, Stage 1 vs Stage 2 routing
and the four PDC design cases. Click anything (equipment, stream, loop, interlock, symbol or legend item) to select it; Ctrl+click adds more; the view zooms to fit and Esc clears.

The header dropdown picks the PDC case (nominal 25.5 Mtpa, design grade 24.0, achievable 25.5, high throughput 28.35). The tph box borders take the selected case colour (red high throughput, orange achievable, yellow nominal, blue design grade), shown as a legend in the dropdown. Mill reline modes are not offered because the PDC gives no flows for them.

Look up (phone home screen, or the PFD header search, press / ): type any part of a tag, line number, valve, hose, SPI or P&ID number, or plain words. Spaces and hyphens are ignored. Each result shows every field from its list, links to anything it mentions, and everything that references it. 📷 reads a tag from a photo using Tesseract.js (downloaded from jsDelivr the first time, then runs on the device).

Where the data lives:

* `pfd-data.js`: layout, routing (PFD, corrected to the PDC where they clash), node notes, control loops (with real instrument tags) and interlocks.
* `pfd-streams.js`: every stream's figures from the Stage 2 PDC (2000-F00-DCR-PR-10002 Rev 4), keyed by stream id with the PDC section number. Four columns: nominal 25.5 Mtpa, design grade 24.0 Mtpa, achievable grade 25.5 Mtpa, high throughput 28.35 Mtpa. Missing water, slurry, SG and m³/h values are calculated in the viewer and shown in blue italics.
* `search-data.json`: every item in the MEL, instrument list, control valve list, manual valve list, line list, SPI list and both slurry hose lists, built by `python3 tools/build_search.py <folder with the .xlsx files>`. Rebuild it when a list revision comes out (files are found by document number).
* Layout: equipment names always sit above their symbol, and lines into or out of the top stop at the name box. Every line with a PDC tonnage shows a tph box, and the layout keeps each line longer than its box. In the browser console, `pfdCheck()` lists any clash (short lines, overlapping names, crossed area titles, jogs). `node tools/relayout.js '[["x",at,amount],["y",at,amount]]'` inserts space like adding blank columns or rows, moving everything beyond that point, including hand-routed lines. Loop and interlock points are pinned to equipment (`[node, side, fraction, distance]`), so they follow any relayout. The areas are arranged in rows to fill a 16:9 screen (2560 × 1440): `node tools/moveframes.js` moves whole areas; lines between areas that moved differently are marked `ar:1` and routed automatically in pfd.html (A* around equipment and name boxes), then saved into the data with `pfdBake()`.
* `pfd-equip.js`: generated by `tools/build_equip.py` from JSON exports of the MEL, instrument list and control valve list (the spreadsheets themselves are not committed). Re-run `node tools/loop_tags.js > tags.txt` then `python3 tools/build_equip.py <json folder> tags.txt` after a list revision.

## Checks page, updates and the phone PFD

* `issues.html` (🐞 Checks, linked from the phone Settings and the PFD sidebar) lists contradictions between the documents: what document 1 says, document 1, what document 2 says, document 2, and which one the app takes as true and why. Rows come from `issues.json` (build with `python3 tools/build_issues.py` after `tools/build_search.py`; the decisions made while building the app are written into that script) plus live checks of the Smart PFD layout and data. Filter by type, category or words, and export what is shown to Excel (`xlsx-lite.js`, works offline). Each row also carries the revision date of both documents. The dates come from each document's revision block (spreadsheet cover pages, the MEL header, drawing title blocks, report revision tables), read by `python3 tools/doc_revs.py <source folder>` into `tools/doc_revs.json`. Where two documents clash, the newer one is taken as true unless the comment says why not; the newer date is marked ★, and the Source documents tab (`issues.html#sources`, also linked as 📄 Sources from the phone Settings and the PFD sidebar) lists every source document: number, title, type, latest revision and date, status, client review stamp, what the app uses it for, and the full revision history, with its own Excel export. Each document also has ▦ View tables, a full screen viewer of every table in it (all rows and columns, Excel row numbers and column letters, filter, big sheets loaded in 5,000 row chunks), and ⬇ Excel, a download of those tables. Both come from `sources/`, built by `python3 tools/build_sources.py <source folder>`: spreadsheets are rebuilt from their cell values without the cover page, logos, comments, file properties, external links or sign off names (hidden sheets stay hidden); PDFs give their data tables (the PDFs are drawings, so sheet, equipment and off sheet connection tables are built from the drawing text). The service worker does not keep `sources/` offline except its index.
* ⟳ Update app (phone Settings, PFD sidebar, Checks page) shows the installed and latest version, clears the offline copy and reloads the newest version.
* On a phone the PFD opens full height at the crushing end, pinch zooms without limit, keeps a compact legend and filter strip along the bottom, shows details as a bottom sheet and puts flows and the full legend in the ☰ drawer.

## Layout view (Smart PFD)

The 🗺 Layout switch in the Smart PFD header shows the same equipment and streams on the real plant, on Google satellite imagery (no labels; Hybrid and Esri as alternatives), loaded through Leaflet the same way as the Photo Map app. Selection, flows, stage, service lines and the info panel are shared with the schematic. Zoomed out it shows the big equipment and the main process lines; zooming in adds small items, names and service lines, and names that would overlap are hidden (selected items first).

Positions come from the two layout drawings, FIM 2 (2000-F00-DRG-GE-10100 Rev 0, new plant) and FIM 1 (2000-F00-DRG-GE-20001 Rev 2, old plant). `tools/build_layout.py` holds each item's position on a sheet and ties both sheets to the ground through three points surveyed in Google Maps (tails thickener centre, COS 2 dome, Fimiston COS dome); it writes `pfd-layout-data.js`. Residuals are about 3 m. Items drawn with an orange dashed outline are estimated because they are not identifiable on either drawing. Anyone can correct a position: open the item, ✥ Move on layout, drag, ✓ Done. Moves are kept on the device and ⬇ Moves downloads them as a small JSON file to build in.
